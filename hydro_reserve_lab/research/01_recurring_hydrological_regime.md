# Claim 1: does the regime genuinely recur, and in a specific, chosen real basin?

**Status: VERIFIED**, both generically and for a specific chosen region — the Colorado River Basin,
selected as this lab's target region for the reasons in `04_colorado_river_economics.md` and
`05_reliability_standard_firm_yield.md` (real, large, well-documented economic stakes; a real
public multi-agency modeling practice to benchmark against; extensive USGS gauge coverage).

## Generic mechanism — multiple independent, real, recurring drivers

- **ENSO (El Niño/La Niña)** drives real, recurring streamflow and groundwater-recharge swings —
  an extreme El Niño can reduce streamflow/recharge by roughly 60% relative to ENSO-neutral
  conditions (NOAA Climate.gov); the US Southwest sees measurably more dry episodes in La Niña
  years than El Niño years.
- A 2024 hydrology paper (*Hydrology and Earth System Sciences*, "On the timescale of drought
  indices for monitoring streamflow drought considering catchment hydrological regimes") documents
  **quantified recurring oscillation cycles between standardized streamflow index and ENSO at two
  distinct timescales: intra-annual (12-48 months) and inter-decadal (128-256 months)** — the
  recurrence is measured, not asserted loosely.
- A 2025 *Scientific Reports* paper ("Atmospheric teleconnection patterns and hydrological
  whiplashes in the Western U.S.") documents **recurring, increasing-frequency shifts between
  extreme wet and dry phases** — current (2025), not a settled historical curiosity, and directly
  the "regime recurs many times, and possibly with a nonstationary trend" shape this litmus test
  cares about.
- PDO (Pacific Decadal Oscillation) is an independently-confirmed second real driver at a longer,
  decadal timescale — i.e. the recurrence isn't hostage to a single climate-oscillation mechanism.

## The specific chosen basin — the Colorado River's real, ongoing "megadrought"

The Colorado River Basin has been in a formally recognized "megadrought" since 2000 — described in
multiple independent sources (Woodwell Climate, Colorado Sun, USBR's own news releases) as **the
worst 23-year period in 1,200 years of tree-ring-reconstructed hydrology**. This is a real,
large-scale, well-documented recurring/persistent dry regime with:
- **Real, dated shortage-tier trigger events** (not a single one-off): Lake Mead crossed the
  Tier 1 shortage threshold (elevation below 1,075 ft) in **August 2021** — the first shortage
  condition ever declared on the reservoir — followed by a **Tier 2 declaration in August 2022**
  (elevation projected below 1,050 ft). Two real, separate, escalating regime-crossing events
  within the same multi-decade dry period, not one instantaneous change.
- Both Lake Mead and Lake Powell fell to **27% full by summer 2022** — the lowest levels ever
  recorded at either reservoir since filling.

This gives `hydro_reserve_lab` a real candidate region with (a) a documented, ongoing recurring/
persistent dry regime, (b) multiple real historical threshold-crossing events to anchor a
regime-mixture model's "did we detect this" question against (unlike `shm_lab`'s single retrofit,
there are at least two real, independently-dated shortage escalations to check detection against,
plus the whole basin's decades-long historical record for the recurring-regime side of the model),
and (c) extensive real economic and reliability-standard grounding (see the next two research
notes).
