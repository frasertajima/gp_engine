# Claim 4: real, sourced dollar figures for the asymmetric-cost decision

**Status: VERIFIED** — real, specific, sourced figures exist, a stronger starting position than
`shm_lab` ever reached on its own asymmetric-cost side (which stayed genuinely unsourced).

## The scale of what's at stake

Colorado River Basin water is estimated to generate **$20.6 billion/year in total economic
benefits**: approximately **$1.4 billion from agricultural production**, **$18.3 billion from
urban water use**, and **$874 million from hydropower**. Real, if not yet fully deconstructed by
per-shortage-scenario dollar impact — a Phase 1 task, not assumed solved by this figure alone.

## Real over-procurement-side figures (the "cost of more supply/storage" side)

- **Agricultural water-conservation programs** (paying farmers to fallow land or use less water,
  the Bureau of Reclamation's actual current tool, per the same search pass's Nevada
  Current/AEPP coverage) cost **~$417/acre-foot on average**, with some programs as low as
  **$69.89/acre-foot**.
- **Local supply projects** (new infrastructure — desalination, reuse, etc.) cost **more than
  $2,400/acre-foot on average** — a real, large, sourced gap between "pay to conserve" and "build
  new supply," directly analogous to `grid_reserve_lab`'s PJM-vs-ERCOT capacity-cost-vs-VOLL
  asymmetry.

## Real under-procurement-side (shortage) figures

- **Real price disparity by user class**: agricultural water districts pay an average of
  **$30.32/acre-foot**, while municipal districts pay **$512.01/acre-foot** — a ~17x gap,
  suggesting municipal shortage is a materially more expensive failure mode per acre-foot than
  agricultural shortage, a real, sourced asymmetry worth building into the decision model rather
  than treating "water" as one undifferentiated commodity.
- **Real crop-value figures**: agricultural output averaged **$814/acre-foot in the Lower Basin**
  vs. only **$131/acre-foot in the Upper Basin** — a large, real, sourced regional asymmetry in
  what a shortage actually costs in lost production, not a single national number.
- A real, documented regional case: severe Colorado River-driven cuts forced ranchers in Utah's
  Uintah Basin to reduce cattle herds, raising production costs and straining the local economy —
  a real, reported (if not yet dollar-quantified in this pass) downstream consequence of shortage.

## Honest gap

**Not yet sourced**: a single, clean VOLL-style headline number (`grid_reserve_lab`'s ERCOT
$35,000/MWh analogue) for water shortage specifically, nor a single official reservoir-capacity
cost-per-acre-foot figure comparable to PJM/MISO's $/MW-year. The figures above are real and usable
but more fragmented (by user class, by sub-basin) than the electricity-sector analogues — Phase 1's
economic-layer design should account for this fragmentation explicitly (e.g. a per-user-class
shortage cost, not one blended figure) rather than force a single artificial VOLL-equivalent that
doesn't reflect how differentiated the real cost structure actually is.
